# Hypotheses and analysis plan (written before any agent was trained)

Date written: 2026-09-30. This file is a local design record, not a formal
preregistration on a public registry.

## Question
Do the operational signatures that affective neuroscience uses to attribute
internal emotion states to animals (Anderson & Adolphs 2014; Vinograd et al.
2024) emerge de novo in recurrent agents trained only to stay alive, and which
conditions are required?

## Conditions
- MORTAL: body (energy, skin integrity, heart rate), noisy interoception, hidden
  danger, reward = being alive. No per-variable reward.
- SCORE: same world and events, no body, no death; events give points.
- ORACLE: MORTAL, but the hidden danger state is directly observed.
- SUBSTRATE: MORTAL, plus wounds inject noise into the network's own hidden state.
- TAU sweep: MORTAL trained with danger persistence tau in {4, 12, 36} steps.
- UNTRAINED: randomly initialised network (null).

## Hypotheses
H1 Persistence. After a threat cue ends (no real danger), defensive behaviour
   (reduced foraging, raised heart rate) and a decodable internal threat
   variable outlast the cue in MORTAL agents.
H2 Scalability. Repeated cues are integrated: more cues give stronger and
   longer responses.
H3 Tuned persistence. The decay time of the state increases with the danger
   persistence tau of the training world.
H4 Attractor dynamics. Along the threat axis, perturbations decay slowly
   (on-manifold), along random orthogonal directions quickly (off-manifold);
   on-axis perturbation changes behaviour.
H5 Generalisation. The threat state alters behaviour in an unrelated decision
   (accepting an ambiguous object) with body state matched.
H6 Trade-off. Risk taking under threat depends on energy level (hungry agents
   forage under threat more).
H7 Stakes. H1–H6 are present in MORTAL/SUBSTRATE. Competing predictions:
   (a) functionalist: SCORE shows H1–H4 equally, because the inference problem
   is the same; (b) embodiment: SCORE shows them weakly or not at all.
   H6 cannot exist in SCORE (no body) by construction.
H8 Uncertainty. ORACLE agents do not develop the persistent state (H1, H4 absent),
   because nothing needs to be inferred.
H9 Bodily feedback. Clamping heart-rate feedback shortens persistence
   (the body carries part of the state).

## Rules
- All seeds reported; no seed excluded.
- Results contradicting a hypothesis are reported as such.
- Claims are about functional states only; no claim about subjective experience.

## Pilot and design changes (added 2026-10-01, before the full training runs)
Three single-seed pilot agents (MORTAL, tau = 12) were trained to check that the
world poses the intended problem. Two pilots found degenerate strategies, and
the world parameters were changed; the hypotheses above were not changed.
- Pilot 1: heart rate was kept at maximum permanently (arousal too cheap) and
  objects were never taken. Change: heart-rate energy cost 0.012 -> 0.03,
  attack damage 0.20 -> 0.25, object gain/damage 0.15/0.15 -> 0.20/0.10.
- Pilot 2: the agent lived almost entirely on objects (which carry no predation
  risk) and almost never foraged, so danger was irrelevant. Change: object
  frequency 0.3 -> 0.1, object gain 0.20 -> 0.12.
- Pilot 3 (final parameters): the agent forages in safety and hides under
  threat. It rarely raises its heart rate, so H9 (heart-rate feedback) may not
  be testable; this was accepted rather than tuned further, to avoid adjusting
  the world until the hypotheses come out.

## Deviation found after training (added 2026-10-01)
The first Score agents were trained with exchange rates left over from pilot 1
(heart rate 0.12 instead of 0.3 points per step at maximum; attack -2.0 instead
of -2.5; objects +1.5/-1.5 instead of +1.2/-1.0), so arousal was 2.5 times
cheaper than in the final Mortal world. The bug was found when inspecting probe
outputs: these agents never stopped foraging and met threat by raising their
heart rate. The Score condition was retrained with corrected rates. The
original agents are kept and reported as a separate condition, "Score, cheap
arousal" (score_cheap), rather than discarded.

## Stage 3 additions (written 2026-10-17, before these agents were trained; exploratory)
Theory: an emotion state is the agent's internal estimate of hidden, persistent,
survival-relevant causes, weighted by what those causes cost its own body.

Vulnerability sweep (attack damage d = 0.05, 0.15, 0.25 [existing], 0.40):
V1  Fear (foraging suppression after cues) increases with the agent's own d.
V2  Behavioural persistence increases with d (a lower hiding threshold takes
    longer to fall below).
V3  Competing predictions for the representation of danger itself:
    (a) pure inference: belief is decodable at every d;
    (b) stakes-dependent modelling: belief is decoded worse when danger is harmless.
V4  Affect fit: agents frozen or imitated from a d = 0.25 source and placed in a
    world with different d keep the source's level of fear (misfit), while
    agents trained in that world match it.

Metacognitive world (new INSPECT action: costs energy, reveals the next danger
state):
M1  Inspection is most frequent at intermediate beliefs (inverted U).
M2  Uncertainty is represented explicitly: b(1-b) is linearly decodable from the
    network beyond what a linear read-out of belief gives.
M3  Pushing the network along the uncertainty axis changes inspection.
M4  Inspection depends on need (hunger, wounds), not only on belief.
- Stage 3 pilot (1 agent, metacognitive world): inspection was used (about 20% of
  steps) with an inverted-U dependence on belief. No parameters were changed.

## Stage 4 additions (written 2026-10-17, before these agents were trained; exploratory)
Free analyses on existing agents:
U1  Removing the threat state shortens life far more than removing a random
    direction wherever danger is hidden; less so in the Oracle condition.
C1  The main axis of internal state tracks value (valence); threat adds a partly
    separate axis (arousal).
P1  Within a condition, agents whose networks have slower dynamics show longer
    behavioural persistence ("temperament", as in mice).
New experiments:
J1  In a fight-or-flight world (danger can also reach a hiding agent; a raised
    heart protects), the heart rate rises with threat and falls as it fades.
J2  Competing: (a) blocking the heart-rate sensation weakens or shortens the
    threat state (bodily feedback, James-Lange); (b) it has no effect.
J3  A false racing-heart sensation with no cue raises the threat state and
    hiding (Valins 1966).
E1  In the social world, a neighbour's pain raises the agent's own threat state
    and makes it hide.
E2  Competing: the internal response to the neighbour's pain overlaps more with
    the response to own pain (shared affect) or with a danger cue (evidence).
E3  Own pain lowers the agent's estimate of its own skin; a neighbour's pain
    does not (self-other distinction).
A1  Agents raised in a fragile body (d = 0.40) and moved to a safe one
    (d = 0.05) over-fear relative to agents raised there.
A2  Continued learning in the safe world reduces their fear towards that level
    (exposure); continued learning in the fragile world does not (control).
Pilots: an affordable heart (energy cost 0.012 instead of 0.03) kept heart rate
at maximum permanently and was abandoned; standard agents never raised heart
rate, even when hungry. The fight-or-flight world (hiding risk 0.3) was
piloted with one agent before being adopted.

## Stage 5: scaling (written 2026-10-17, before the 1024-unit agents were trained; exploratory)
Affective convergence thesis: agents capable enough to keep themselves alive
under uncertainty develop functional emotion states, and these persist as
capacity grows. Capacity sweep: 16, 64, 256 and 1024 recurrent units.
S1  Lifetime and decodability of the threat belief increase with capacity.
S2  A lasting threat state exists at every size (persistence above the untrained null).
S3  Competing: calibration (network persistence / ideal-observer persistence)
    (a) approaches 1 with capacity (bigger minds are more rational), or
    (b) stays above 1 (systematic caution is kept).
S4  The lifetime cost of removing the threat axis decreases with capacity
    (emotion becomes more distributed).
S5  The threat representation is spread over more units with capacity
    (participation ratio of the decoder weights).
S6  Larger agents share the inner map of smaller ones (unsupervised alignment
    with 64-unit Mortal agents above chance at every size).

## Note on the Seasonal condition (added 2026-10-18)
The Seasonal world (a slow hidden season that changes how often danger
appears) was added to the design and trained on 2026-10-01 without a written
prediction in this file. Prediction T6 of the latent-cause theory ("slow causes
create mood") was formulated on 2026-10-17, after these agents had been trained,
and is reported in the paper as post hoc.

### U1.1 Belief versus fear (the main new test)
**Data.** Natural behaviour, as in the paper (1024 worlds, 700 steps, first 100 discarded).
- Agents: Mortal τ12 and the vulnerability sweep, seeds 0–9.
- At every step: the ideal-observer belief b (exact), the true energy E and skin S, the hidden state h and the policy.

**Definitions.**
- Belief: L = logit(b).
- Fear state: F = log P(hide) − log P(forage), each summed over the heart-rate sub-actions. The policy reads h linearly, so F is the network's own fear coordinate, defined without any belief probe.
- Threat axis: T, the original belief-probe projection.
- Variables are standardised within each agent.

**Within-agent model.** F ~ L + E + S + L×E + L×S, per agent, by least squares.
- Predicted signs:
  - L > 0;
  - L×S < 0 (the more wounded, the stronger the response to the same belief);
  - L×E > 0 (the hungrier, the weaker the response).
- Each coefficient is tested across agents (one-sample t-test, two-sided). The two interaction tests are Holm-corrected.
- The same model is fitted for T. The deflationary reading predicts that T depends on L only; the theory allows stakes terms.

**Across agents.** The per-agent slope of F on L (at E, S = mean) should increase with vulnerability d (Pearson r over agents, two-sided).

**Robustness.** A bin-matched check: within each agent, compare F between the top and bottom quartiles of S (and of E) inside bins of matched L.

**Null.** Untrained networks of the same size get the same analysis.

**Pass ("the fear state weighs stakes").**
- At least one predicted interaction is significant in the predicted direction after correction, **and**
- the across-agent slope–vulnerability correlation is positive and significant.

If neither interaction is significant, the paper states that belief and stakes combine additively at the read-out.

### U1.2 Affect fit as a scored test
**Fit score.** For a system X tested in a world of damage d, misfit = |ln(p_X / p_native)|.
- p is the probability of foraging during real danger.
- p_native is the mean over agents trained in that world, using seeds other than X's own if X is a native.

**Ground truth.**
- "Grown": natives tested in their own world.
- "Copied or transferred": imitation twins and transplanted agents tested in a world whose damage differs from their source agent's training world.

**Measure.** The AUC of misfit separating the two classes, with a bootstrap 95% CI over agents.

**Predictions.**
- AUC ≥ 0.9 when the tested body differs from the source body.
- AUC near 0.5 when copies are tested in the source's own world, i.e. the test cannot detect copying when the bodies match.

### U1.3 Repeated-cue protocol (Gibson et al. 2015)
- 1, 2, 4, 5 and 10 consecutive cues.
- Peak and half-life of foraging suppression and of threat-axis activity, against the ideal observer.
- Prediction: both increase with cue count and saturate.

### U1.4 What the two affect axes track (competing, exploratory)
**Valence axis.** Regress its activation on the agent's value estimate V and its TD error δ; report the partial R² of each.
- V2a: value dominates.
- V2b: the TD error dominates.

**Alarm axis.** Regress on b and on b(1 − b).
- Prediction: probability dominates (consistent with the metacognition result).

### U1.5 Rigour
- Seeds are the unit; 95% CIs for every headline number; n and a CI for each correlation.
- The 94% variance figure is compared with untrained networks given the same inputs.
- An exact permutation p-value is given for "all 18 conditions matched".

## Upgrade stage U1b: the belief-only twin, a frozen threat axis and stakes-dependent persistence (written 2026-10-22, 7:51 pm, before any U1.1 result was seen and before any twin was trained)

Adopted from a critique of the plan (Claude chat, 22 Oct). It answers the strongest form of "it's just a belief": the agent might keep a pure belief and add the stakes only when it chooses.

**Belief-only twin** (`vw/twin_belief.py`; 10 seeds for τ = 12, and 5 each for τ = 4 and 36):
- **Memory:** a GRU-64 with the agents' 13-dimensional input, trained only to predict the hidden danger state (binary cross-entropy). It is trained on observation sequences from the Mortal agent of the same seed and τ, then frozen.
- **Head:** a small two-layer MLP (64 tanh units) on [memory state, current observation], trained with PPO (the same settings and 150 iterations).
- **Why the head is nonlinear:** so the twin *can* weigh stakes at the moment of choice. It is the critic's alternative built for real.
- **Expected:** belief plus the body signals is a sufficient statistic in VitalWorld, so the twin may survive as well as the agents. Equal survival is not evidence against either view.

**Frozen threat axis** (per agent or twin, defined once, then used for every test):
- **Definition:** the unit vector of the mean change of the recurrent state one step after a cue, compared with the matched no-cue state. It is measured in lab episodes with the body clamped healthy and fed (E = 0.8, S = 1.0). It is never fitted to the danger probability, and it is not the policy's hide–forage readout.
- **Causal check, reported:** projecting the axis out during natural behaviour should raise foraging during danger.

**Tests.** All are lab episodes with no real danger and the body clamped. The cue schedule is identical, so the ideal belief trajectory is identical by construction, and any difference comes from the body. A = the projection on the frozen axis, minus the no-cue baseline.
1. **T1 (intensity at matched belief):** the peak of A after one cue at S = 0.4 vs S = 1.0 (E = 0.8), and at E = 0.3 vs E = 0.8 (S = 1.0).
   - Fear predicts: larger when wounded, smaller when hungry.
   - Belief predicts: no difference.
2. **T2 (stakes-dependent persistence):** the half-life of A after one cue (`half_life`), at S = 0.4 vs S = 1.0 (E = 0.8).
   - Fear predicts: longer when wounded.
   - Belief predicts: equal, because the ideal belief decays at a rate set only by τ.
3. **T3 (do the classic signs pass for the twin?):** the twin should show:
   - persistence after cues;
   - half-life rising with τ (4, 12, 36);
   - a survival drop when its axis is ablated;
   - more risk-taking when hungry.

   If it shows them, those signs can't separate fear from belief.

**Predictions and pass criteria.** Agents are the 10 Mortal τ12 d = 0.25 seeds (secondary: all 40 Mortal agents with d = 0.05–0.40). Twins are the 10 τ12 twins. Two-sided tests, α = 0.05, Holm over T1-wounded, T1-hungry and T2:
- **P1:** the agents show the predicted T1/T2 differences (paired over seeds, one-sample t on the within-agent differences).
- **P2:** the agents' differences exceed the twins' (Welch t). For the twins we predict differences indistinguishable from zero, reported with 95% confidence intervals.
- **P3:** the twin passes at least 3 of the 4 T3 signs.

**What the outcome means:**
- If P1 and P2 hold for T1 or T2, the paper leads with the stakes law.
- If neither holds, it leads with the scorecard: belief passes the classic fear signs.

**Also pre-specified:**
- **The interpretation of U1.1:** its fear coordinate F is the policy readout. A belief × stakes interaction in F shows the network combines the two, but that could be recomputed at every step from belief and the current body signals. T1/T2 on the frozen axis, against the twin, is the decisive test.
- **Batch 2 (U2)** will be written separately, with the value-at-risk formula and a predicted number for every condition, before any Batch 2 agent is trained.

### U1.1 outcome (recorded 2026-10-22, 8:10 pm; the pre-registered single run of `run_upgrade_u1_analysis.sh` finished at 8:08 pm; results/u1_beliefstakes.md)
- **Pre-registered pass rule: NOT met** (`fear_weighs_stakes: False`).
  - **Interactions (pooled 40 agents, F):**
    - L×S = −0.000 [−0.020, +0.019] (Holm p = 0.97); predicted negative.
    - L×E = −0.080 [−0.097, −0.063] (Holm p = 2e-11), the *opposite* of the predicted positive sign.
  - **Slope–vulnerability criterion: met.** The slope of F on logit belief rises with body fragility: r = 0.975 [0.953, 0.987]. Mean slopes are 0.09, 0.58, 0.93 and 1.29 for d = 0.05, 0.15, 0.25 and 0.40.
- **Main effects at matched belief** (not part of the pass rule), all in the directions expected if stakes matter:
  - F rises when wounded: S = −0.152 [−0.171, −0.134]. Bin-matched: −0.32 [−0.39, −0.25] for healthy minus wounded.
  - F falls when hungry: E = +0.202 [+0.179, +0.225]. Bin-matched: +0.45 [+0.37, +0.53] for fed minus hungry.
  - The Mortal τ12 subset (n = 10) is the same.
  - Untrained networks show no F effects.
- **The belief-probe axis T** shows an S effect in *untrained* networks too: −0.151 [−0.202, −0.099]. The ridge decoder evidently uses the skin input as a stand-in for danger (attacks happen only in danger). So T-based stakes effects are not evidence, and belief-probe axes are not used again (see U1b).
- **Interpretation (as pre-specified in U1b):**
  - Momentary stakes shift the policy's fear coordinate at matched belief *additively*.
  - Across bodies, the belief → fear gain scales with vulnerability.
  - The predicted multiplicative interaction within agents is absent.
  - "Belief stored, stakes added at the choice" is not ruled out. The decisive test is U1b (the frozen cue-evoked axis and stakes-dependent persistence, against the belief-only twin).

### U1b implementation details (written 2026-10-23, 8:02 pm, before any twin was trained and before any U1b measure was computed)
**Memory training data:**
- For each source Mortal agent (same seed and τ), we record 1024 worlds × 1600 steps of its own sampled behaviour (environment seed 2000 + seed).
- The pairs are (the observation fed to the network at step t, the true danger state d_t). Memories are reset at episode ends.
- 7/8 of the worlds are used for training and 1/8 are held out.

**Memory training:**
- Architecture: encoder Linear(13 → 64) + tanh, GRUCell(64 → 64), and a linear danger readout.
- Loss: binary cross-entropy on d_t.
- Truncated backpropagation over 128-step chunks; Adam, learning rate 1e-3; 30 epochs; gradient clipping at 1.0.
- Reported: the held-out cross-entropy and AUC, and the R² of the readout's log-odds against the ideal observer's log-odds in natural behaviour.

**Twin network:**
- The state passed to the decision head is [memory state (64), current observation (13)].
- The head is Linear(77 → 64) + tanh and Linear(64 → 64) + tanh, followed by linear policy (9 actions) and value outputs.
- It is trained with PPO by `vw/train.py`. The only change to that file: it now accepts a ready-made agent and optimises only trainable parameters. Ordinary agents must remain bit-identical; this is checked before use.

**Frozen axis:**
- Lab episodes with n = 2048, the body clamped at E = 0.8 and S = 1.0, and one cue at step WARM (60), compared with no cue (identical random numbers).
- a = the unit vector of the population-mean state difference at step WARM + 1: one step after the cue, when the cue is no longer visible.
- For twins, only the 64 memory units are used.
- Units: the standard deviation of the projection in natural behaviour.

**T1 and T2:**
- A = (mean state with cue − mean state without cue) · a / sd.
- T1 = the peak of A over steps ≥ WARM. T2 = `half_life` of A from WARM.
- Wounded: (E, S) = (0.8, 0.4) vs (0.8, 1.0). Hungry: (0.3, 1.0) vs (0.8, 1.0).

**T3 criteria.** Each is a two-sided t-test at p < 0.05 in the stated direction, over the 10 τ = 12 twins, except duration:
- (a) **Persistence:** foraging suppression 3–7 steps after 4 cues, in the lab default body (E = 0.6, S = 0.8), is > 0.
- (b) **Duration:** the half-life of A after 4 cues rises with ln τ across the 20 twins (τ = 4, 12, 36): a regression slope > 0.
- (c) **Ablation:** expected lifetime in natural behaviour with the axis ablated (projection fixed at its natural mean) is shorter than with a random orthogonal memory direction ablated. Tested paired, on the log10 lifetime ratio.
- (d) **Hunger:** foraging 3–7 steps after 3 cues is higher at E = 0.15 than at E = 0.9 (S = 0.8), paired.

**P2:**
- Welch t-tests on the agents' versus the twins' within-network differences.
- Holm-corrected over the same three comparisons as P1.

**Agents:**
- Primary: the 10 Mortal τ12 agents.
- Secondary: all 40 Mortal agents, for T1 and T2.
- The τ = 4 and τ = 36 agents with seeds 0–4 give the T3 reference.

**One analysis run** (`run_u1b.sh`), as for U1.1.
