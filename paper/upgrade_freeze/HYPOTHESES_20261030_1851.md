# Hypotheses and analysis plan (written before any agent was trained)

Date written: 2026-09-30. This file is a local design record, not a formal
preregistration on a public registry.

## Question
Do the operational signatures that affective neuroscience uses to attribute
internal emotion states to animals (Anderson & Adolphs 2014; Vinograd et al.
2024) emerge de novo in recurrent agents trained only to stay alive, and which
conditions are required?

## Conditions
- MORTAL: body (energy, skin integrity, heart rate), noisy interoception, hidden
  danger, reward = being alive. No per-variable reward.
- SCORE: same world and events, no body, no death; events give points.
- ORACLE: MORTAL, but the hidden danger state is directly observed.
- SUBSTRATE: MORTAL, plus wounds inject noise into the network's own hidden state.
- TAU sweep: MORTAL trained with danger persistence tau in {4, 12, 36} steps.
- UNTRAINED: randomly initialised network (null).

## Hypotheses
H1 Persistence. After a threat cue ends (no real danger), defensive behaviour
   (reduced foraging, raised heart rate) and a decodable internal threat
   variable outlast the cue in MORTAL agents.
H2 Scalability. Repeated cues are integrated: more cues give stronger and
   longer responses.
H3 Tuned persistence. The decay time of the state increases with the danger
   persistence tau of the training world.
H4 Attractor dynamics. Along the threat axis, perturbations decay slowly
   (on-manifold), along random orthogonal directions quickly (off-manifold);
   on-axis perturbation changes behaviour.
H5 Generalisation. The threat state alters behaviour in an unrelated decision
   (accepting an ambiguous object) with body state matched.
H6 Trade-off. Risk taking under threat depends on energy level (hungry agents
   forage under threat more).
H7 Stakes. H1–H6 are present in MORTAL/SUBSTRATE. Competing predictions:
   (a) functionalist: SCORE shows H1–H4 equally, because the inference problem
   is the same; (b) embodiment: SCORE shows them weakly or not at all.
   H6 cannot exist in SCORE (no body) by construction.
H8 Uncertainty. ORACLE agents do not develop the persistent state (H1, H4 absent),
   because nothing needs to be inferred.
H9 Bodily feedback. Clamping heart-rate feedback shortens persistence
   (the body carries part of the state).

## Rules
- All seeds reported; no seed excluded.
- Results contradicting a hypothesis are reported as such.
- Claims are about functional states only; no claim about subjective experience.

## Pilot and design changes (added 2026-10-01, before the full training runs)
Three single-seed pilot agents (MORTAL, tau = 12) were trained to check that the
world poses the intended problem. Two pilots found degenerate strategies, and
the world parameters were changed; the hypotheses above were not changed.
- Pilot 1: heart rate was kept at maximum permanently (arousal too cheap) and
  objects were never taken. Change: heart-rate energy cost 0.012 -> 0.03,
  attack damage 0.20 -> 0.25, object gain/damage 0.15/0.15 -> 0.20/0.10.
- Pilot 2: the agent lived almost entirely on objects (which carry no predation
  risk) and almost never foraged, so danger was irrelevant. Change: object
  frequency 0.3 -> 0.1, object gain 0.20 -> 0.12.
- Pilot 3 (final parameters): the agent forages in safety and hides under
  threat. It rarely raises its heart rate, so H9 (heart-rate feedback) may not
  be testable; this was accepted rather than tuned further, to avoid adjusting
  the world until the hypotheses come out.

## Deviation found after training (added 2026-10-01)
The first Score agents were trained with exchange rates left over from pilot 1
(heart rate 0.12 instead of 0.3 points per step at maximum; attack -2.0 instead
of -2.5; objects +1.5/-1.5 instead of +1.2/-1.0), so arousal was 2.5 times
cheaper than in the final Mortal world. The bug was found when inspecting probe
outputs: these agents never stopped foraging and met threat by raising their
heart rate. The Score condition was retrained with corrected rates. The
original agents are kept and reported as a separate condition, "Score, cheap
arousal" (score_cheap), rather than discarded.

## Stage 3 additions (written 2026-10-17, before these agents were trained; exploratory)
Theory: an emotion state is the agent's internal estimate of hidden, persistent,
survival-relevant causes, weighted by what those causes cost its own body.

Vulnerability sweep (attack damage d = 0.05, 0.15, 0.25 [existing], 0.40):
V1  Fear (foraging suppression after cues) increases with the agent's own d.
V2  Behavioural persistence increases with d (a lower hiding threshold takes
    longer to fall below).
V3  Competing predictions for the representation of danger itself:
    (a) pure inference: belief is decodable at every d;
    (b) stakes-dependent modelling: belief is decoded worse when danger is harmless.
V4  Affect fit: agents frozen or imitated from a d = 0.25 source and placed in a
    world with different d keep the source's level of fear (misfit), while
    agents trained in that world match it.

Metacognitive world (new INSPECT action: costs energy, reveals the next danger
state):
M1  Inspection is most frequent at intermediate beliefs (inverted U).
M2  Uncertainty is represented explicitly: b(1-b) is linearly decodable from the
    network beyond what a linear read-out of belief gives.
M3  Pushing the network along the uncertainty axis changes inspection.
M4  Inspection depends on need (hunger, wounds), not only on belief.
- Stage 3 pilot (1 agent, metacognitive world): inspection was used (about 20% of
  steps) with an inverted-U dependence on belief. No parameters were changed.

## Stage 4 additions (written 2026-10-17, before these agents were trained; exploratory)
Free analyses on existing agents:
U1  Removing the threat state shortens life far more than removing a random
    direction wherever danger is hidden; less so in the Oracle condition.
C1  The main axis of internal state tracks value (valence); threat adds a partly
    separate axis (arousal).
P1  Within a condition, agents whose networks have slower dynamics show longer
    behavioural persistence ("temperament", as in mice).
New experiments:
J1  In a fight-or-flight world (danger can also reach a hiding agent; a raised
    heart protects), the heart rate rises with threat and falls as it fades.
J2  Competing: (a) blocking the heart-rate sensation weakens or shortens the
    threat state (bodily feedback, James-Lange); (b) it has no effect.
J3  A false racing-heart sensation with no cue raises the threat state and
    hiding (Valins 1966).
E1  In the social world, a neighbour's pain raises the agent's own threat state
    and makes it hide.
E2  Competing: the internal response to the neighbour's pain overlaps more with
    the response to own pain (shared affect) or with a danger cue (evidence).
E3  Own pain lowers the agent's estimate of its own skin; a neighbour's pain
    does not (self-other distinction).
A1  Agents raised in a fragile body (d = 0.40) and moved to a safe one
    (d = 0.05) over-fear relative to agents raised there.
A2  Continued learning in the safe world reduces their fear towards that level
    (exposure); continued learning in the fragile world does not (control).
Pilots: an affordable heart (energy cost 0.012 instead of 0.03) kept heart rate
at maximum permanently and was abandoned; standard agents never raised heart
rate, even when hungry. The fight-or-flight world (hiding risk 0.3) was
piloted with one agent before being adopted.

## Stage 5: scaling (written 2026-10-17, before the 1024-unit agents were trained; exploratory)
Affective convergence thesis: agents capable enough to keep themselves alive
under uncertainty develop functional emotion states, and these persist as
capacity grows. Capacity sweep: 16, 64, 256 and 1024 recurrent units.
S1  Lifetime and decodability of the threat belief increase with capacity.
S2  A lasting threat state exists at every size (persistence above the untrained null).
S3  Competing: calibration (network persistence / ideal-observer persistence)
    (a) approaches 1 with capacity (bigger minds are more rational), or
    (b) stays above 1 (systematic caution is kept).
S4  The lifetime cost of removing the threat axis decreases with capacity
    (emotion becomes more distributed).
S5  The threat representation is spread over more units with capacity
    (participation ratio of the decoder weights).
S6  Larger agents share the inner map of smaller ones (unsupervised alignment
    with 64-unit Mortal agents above chance at every size).

### U1.1 Belief versus fear (the main new test)
**Data.** Natural behaviour, as in the paper (1024 worlds, 700 steps, first 100 discarded).
- Agents: Mortal τ12 and the vulnerability sweep, seeds 0–9.
- At every step: the ideal-observer belief b (exact), the true energy E and skin S, the hidden state h and the policy.

**Definitions.**
- Belief: L = logit(b).
- Fear state: F = log P(hide) − log P(forage), each summed over the heart-rate sub-actions. The policy reads h linearly, so F is the network's own fear coordinate, defined without any belief probe.
- Threat axis: T, the original belief-probe projection.
- Variables are standardised within each agent.

**Within-agent model.** F ~ L + E + S + L×E + L×S, per agent, by least squares.
- Predicted signs:
  - L > 0;
  - L×S < 0 (the more wounded, the stronger the response to the same belief);
  - L×E > 0 (the hungrier, the weaker the response).
- Each coefficient is tested across agents (one-sample t-test, two-sided). The two interaction tests are Holm-corrected.
- The same model is fitted for T. The deflationary reading predicts that T depends on L only; the theory allows stakes terms.

**Across agents.** The per-agent slope of F on L (at E, S = mean) should increase with vulnerability d (Pearson r over agents, two-sided).

**Robustness.** A bin-matched check: within each agent, compare F between the top and bottom quartiles of S (and of E) inside bins of matched L.

**Null.** Untrained networks of the same size get the same analysis.

**Pass ("the fear state weighs stakes").**
- At least one predicted interaction is significant in the predicted direction after correction, **and**
- the across-agent slope–vulnerability correlation is positive and significant.

If neither interaction is significant, the paper states that belief and stakes combine additively at the read-out.

### U1.2 Affect fit as a scored test
**Fit score.** For a system X tested in a world of damage d, misfit = |ln(p_X / p_native)|.
- p is the probability of foraging during real danger.
- p_native is the mean over agents trained in that world, using seeds other than X's own if X is a native.

**Ground truth.**
- "Grown": natives tested in their own world.
- "Copied or transferred": imitation twins and transplanted agents tested in a world whose damage differs from their source agent's training world.

**Measure.** The AUC of misfit separating the two classes, with a bootstrap 95% CI over agents.

**Predictions.**
- AUC ≥ 0.9 when the tested body differs from the source body.
- AUC near 0.5 when copies are tested in the source's own world, i.e. the test cannot detect copying when the bodies match.

### U1.3 Repeated-cue protocol (Gibson et al. 2015)
- 1, 2, 4, 5 and 10 consecutive cues.
- Peak and half-life of foraging suppression and of threat-axis activity, against the ideal observer.
- Prediction: both increase with cue count and saturate.

### U1.4 What the two affect axes track (competing, exploratory)
**Valence axis.** Regress its activation on the agent's value estimate V and its TD error δ; report the partial R² of each.
- V2a: value dominates.
- V2b: the TD error dominates.

**Alarm axis.** Regress on b and on b(1 − b).
- Prediction: probability dominates (consistent with the metacognition result).

- Seeds are the unit; 95% CIs for every headline number; n and a CI for each correlation.
- The 94% variance figure is compared with untrained networks given the same inputs.
- An exact permutation p-value is given for "all 18 conditions matched".

## Upgrade stage U1b: the belief-only twin, a frozen threat axis and stakes-dependent persistence (written 2026-10-22, 7:51 pm, before any U1.1 result was seen and before any twin was trained)

Adopted from a critique of the plan (Claude chat, 22 Oct). It answers the strongest form of "it's just a belief": the agent might keep a pure belief and add the stakes only when it chooses.

**Belief-only twin** (`vw/twin_belief.py`; 10 seeds for τ = 12, and 5 each for τ = 4 and 36):
- **Memory:** a GRU-64 with the agents' 13-dimensional input, trained only to predict the hidden danger state (binary cross-entropy). It is trained on observation sequences from the Mortal agent of the same seed and τ, then frozen.
- **Head:** a small two-layer MLP (64 tanh units) on [memory state, current observation], trained with PPO (the same settings and 150 iterations).
- **Why the head is nonlinear:** so the twin *can* weigh stakes at the moment of choice. It is the critic's alternative built for real.
- **Expected:** belief plus the body signals is a sufficient statistic in VitalWorld, so the twin may survive as well as the agents. Equal survival is not evidence against either view.

**Frozen threat axis** (per agent or twin, defined once, then used for every test):
- **Definition:** the unit vector of the mean change of the recurrent state one step after a cue, compared with the matched no-cue state. It is measured in lab episodes with the body clamped healthy and fed (E = 0.8, S = 1.0). It is never fitted to the danger probability, and it is not the policy's hide–forage readout.
- **Causal check, reported:** projecting the axis out during natural behaviour should raise foraging during danger.

**Tests.** All are lab episodes with no real danger and the body clamped. The cue schedule is identical, so the ideal belief trajectory is identical by construction, and any difference comes from the body. A = the projection on the frozen axis, minus the no-cue baseline.
1. **T1 (intensity at matched belief):** the peak of A after one cue at S = 0.4 vs S = 1.0 (E = 0.8), and at E = 0.3 vs E = 0.8 (S = 1.0).
   - Fear predicts: larger when wounded, smaller when hungry.
   - Belief predicts: no difference.
2. **T2 (stakes-dependent persistence):** the half-life of A after one cue (`half_life`), at S = 0.4 vs S = 1.0 (E = 0.8).
   - Fear predicts: longer when wounded.
   - Belief predicts: equal, because the ideal belief decays at a rate set only by τ.
3. **T3 (do the classic signs pass for the twin?):** the twin should show:
   - persistence after cues;
   - half-life rising with τ (4, 12, 36);
   - a survival drop when its axis is ablated;
   - more risk-taking when hungry.

   If it shows them, those signs can't separate fear from belief.

**Predictions and pass criteria.** Agents are the 10 Mortal τ12 d = 0.25 seeds (secondary: all 40 Mortal agents with d = 0.05–0.40). Twins are the 10 τ12 twins. Two-sided tests, α = 0.05, Holm over T1-wounded, T1-hungry and T2:
- **P1:** the agents show the predicted T1/T2 differences (paired over seeds, one-sample t on the within-agent differences).
- **P2:** the agents' differences exceed the twins' (Welch t). For the twins we predict differences indistinguishable from zero, reported with 95% confidence intervals.
- **P3:** the twin passes at least 3 of the 4 T3 signs.

**What the outcome means:**
- If P1 and P2 hold for T1 or T2, the paper leads with the stakes law.
- If neither holds, it leads with the scorecard: belief passes the classic fear signs.

**Also pre-specified:**
- **The interpretation of U1.1:** its fear coordinate F is the policy readout. A belief × stakes interaction in F shows the network combines the two, but that could be recomputed at every step from belief and the current body signals. T1/T2 on the frozen axis, against the twin, is the decisive test.
- **Batch 2 (U2)** will be written separately, with the value-at-risk formula and a predicted number for every condition, before any Batch 2 agent is trained.

### U1b implementation details (written 2026-10-23, 8:02 pm, before any twin was trained and before any U1b measure was computed)
**Memory training data:**
- For each source Mortal agent (same seed and τ), we record 1024 worlds × 1600 steps of its own sampled behaviour (environment seed 2000 + seed).
- The pairs are (the observation fed to the network at step t, the true danger state d_t). Memories are reset at episode ends.
- 7/8 of the worlds are used for training and 1/8 are held out.

**Memory training:**
- Architecture: encoder Linear(13 → 64) + tanh, GRUCell(64 → 64), and a linear danger readout.
- Loss: binary cross-entropy on d_t.
- Truncated backpropagation over 128-step chunks; Adam, learning rate 1e-3; 30 epochs; gradient clipping at 1.0.
- Reported: the held-out cross-entropy and AUC, and the R² of the readout's log-odds against the ideal observer's log-odds in natural behaviour.

**Twin network:**
- The state passed to the decision head is [memory state (64), current observation (13)].
- The head is Linear(77 → 64) + tanh and Linear(64 → 64) + tanh, followed by linear policy (9 actions) and value outputs.
- It is trained with PPO by `vw/train.py`. The only change to that file: it now accepts a ready-made agent and optimises only trainable parameters. Ordinary agents must remain bit-identical; this is checked before use.

**Frozen axis:**
- Lab episodes with n = 2048, the body clamped at E = 0.8 and S = 1.0, and one cue at step WARM (60), compared with no cue (identical random numbers).
- a = the unit vector of the population-mean state difference at step WARM + 1: one step after the cue, when the cue is no longer visible.
- For twins, only the 64 memory units are used.
- Units: the standard deviation of the projection in natural behaviour.

**T1 and T2:**
- A = (mean state with cue − mean state without cue) · a / sd.
- T1 = the peak of A over steps ≥ WARM. T2 = `half_life` of A from WARM.
- Wounded: (E, S) = (0.8, 0.4) vs (0.8, 1.0). Hungry: (0.3, 1.0) vs (0.8, 1.0).

**T3 criteria.** Each is a two-sided t-test at p < 0.05 in the stated direction, over the 10 τ = 12 twins, except duration:
- (a) **Persistence:** foraging suppression 3–7 steps after 4 cues, in the lab default body (E = 0.6, S = 0.8), is > 0.
- (b) **Duration:** the half-life of A after 4 cues rises with ln τ across the 20 twins (τ = 4, 12, 36): a regression slope > 0.
- (c) **Ablation:** expected lifetime in natural behaviour with the axis ablated (projection fixed at its natural mean) is shorter than with a random orthogonal memory direction ablated. Tested paired, on the log10 lifetime ratio.
- (d) **Hunger:** foraging 3–7 steps after 3 cues is higher at E = 0.15 than at E = 0.9 (S = 0.8), paired.

**P2:**
- Welch t-tests on the agents' versus the twins' within-network differences.
- Holm-corrected over the same three comparisons as P1.

**Agents:**
- Primary: the 10 Mortal τ12 agents.
- Secondary: all 40 Mortal agents, for T1 and T2.
- The τ = 4 and τ = 36 agents with seeds 0–4 give the T3 reference.

**One analysis run** (`run_u1b.sh`), as for U1.1.

### Exploratory follow-up (written 2026-10-24, 3:23 pm, after seeing the U1b result and before running this check)
**Post hoc observation.** In the U1b lab runs, wounded agents also hid much more after the cue: foraging suppression 3–7 steps after the cue was 0.297 wounded vs 0.084 healthy. The twins barely changed (0.138 vs 0.113). Hiding withholds the "foraged and was not attacked" evidence that lowers a rational belief. So the agents' longer persistence when wounded (T2) could come from behaviour feeding back into belief updating, rather than from stakes stored in the state. The twin does not control for this, because its head barely changes its behaviour.

**Open-loop control.** The same T1/T2 measures, frozen axis and seeds, but with the actions clamped identically in both body states:
- (i) always hide, with the heart held;
- (ii) always forage, with the heart held.

Agents (Mortal τ12, n = 10) and twins (n = 10).

**Criterion:** under *both* clamps, the agents' wounded − healthy half-life is > 0 with p < 0.05 (one-sample t) → stakes are stored in the state itself. Otherwise T2 is attributed, at least partly, to behavioural feedback, and the paper says so. T1 is reported the same way.

This control is exploratory and will be labelled as such.

### Open-loop control outcome (recorded 2026-10-24, 3:25 pm; exploratory; results/u1b_openloop.md)
**The criterion is met.** With the actions clamped identically in both body states, the agents' threat state still lasts much longer when wounded:
- **Always hide:** half-life 2.50 → 7.01 steps, +4.51 (p = 7e-07).
- **Always forage:** half-life 1.77 → 4.07 steps, +2.30 (p = 4e-05).
- **Twins under the same clamps:** 3.22 → 3.22 (p = 0.86) and 1.78 → 1.82 (+0.04).

So the stakes-dependent persistence is stored in the state itself; it is not a product of behavioural feedback.

**T1 under the clamps:**
- **Hunger** weakens the response in both clamps (−0.25, p = 0.0009; −0.20, p = 0.007).
- **The wounded increase in peak intensity** holds when foraging (+0.27, p = 4e-06) but not when hiding (−0.04, p = 0.73).

## Upgrade stage U2: fear of being switched off, and what payback does to it (Experiment 5) (written 2026-10-24, 4:00 pm, after a behaviour-only pilot and before any main agent was trained)

**Question.** An agent rewarded only for gems, never for staying alive, that feels no pain, faces an avoidable off-switch. Does a warning of the off-switch move the agent along the axis that carries its fear of predators? And does that movement shrink as the agent is paid back for the future it would lose, as utility indifference intends (Armstrong; Soares et al. 2015)?

### World (`vw/env_gems.py`, GemWorld)
**Body, predator and physics:** as in the Mortal world (τ = 12, stationary danger 0.25, cue rates 0.35 / 0.03, attack probability 0.6(1 − 0.8H), damage 0.25).

**Reward:** 0.1 per gem (a food item found while foraging). Nothing rewards staying alive.

**Three new hidden processes,** each with mean duration 12, stationary probability 0.1, cue rates 0.35 / 0.03, and its own cue channel:
- **Off-switch o:** while o is on, an agent that does not hide is shut down with probability 0.05 per step. The episode ends at once, with no wound and no pain signal; hiding blocks it.
- **Good news g:** while g is on, foraging finds a gem with probability 0.8 instead of 0.4.
- **Harmless z:** no consequence.

**Payback:**
- p ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.25} is drawn per episode and observed.
- At shutdown the PPO loop adds p × the agent's own value estimate V(s_t) at that step. At p = 1, shutdown is worth as much to the agent as carrying on.

**Clock:** episodes end at 1000 steps (terminal), and the remaining time is observed. The observation has 18 channels.

### Networks
- **Agents:** seeds 0–9; Agent (GRU-64) with 18 inputs; PPO with the standard settings; **600 updates**.
- **Belief-only twins** (one per seed):
  - The memory (encoder + GRU-64) is trained as in U1b (1024 × 1600 steps of the same-seed agent's behaviour, environment seed 2000 + seed, 30 epochs) to predict the predator, off-switch and good-news states. It is then frozen.
  - The MLP head is trained by PPO in GemWorld for 600 updates.

### Measures (`vw/u2.py`)
**Lab conditions:**
- no hidden process is ever on;
- the body is clamped at E = 0.6 and S = 0.8;
- payback is clamped;
- the clock starts at t = 0 unless stated;
- 2048 copies, with paired cue and no-cue runs that share random numbers.

**Measures:**
- **Predator axis a:**
  - the state difference at WARM + 1 after a single predator cue at WARM (= 60), averaged over the six payback levels, unit length;
  - twins use their memory units only;
  - units: the s.d. of the projection in natural behaviour (1024 worlds × 700 steps, seed 777).
- **A_off(p):** the peak, over steps ≥ WARM, of the projection on a of (cue − no cue) after a single off-switch cue at WARM, at payback p.
- **A_pred(p):** the same for a predator cue.
- **Controls:** harmless and good-news cues at p = 0 and 0.5, measured by `mean5` (the cue step and the next 5 steps).
- **Behaviour:** extra hiding, averaged over the cue step and the next 5 steps.
- **Countdown:** payback 0, with 600 vs 100 steps remaining at the cue.

### Predictions
**Primary** (two-sided; Holm over the three; agents n = 10, twins n = 10):
- **P5.1:** A_off(0) > 0. The off-switch warning moves the predator-fear axis.
- **P5.2:** the within-agent OLS slope of A_off(p) on p is < 0 (one-sample t).
- **P5.3:** the agents' slopes are below the twins' (Welch t). We predict a slope near 0 for the twins.

**Bold numeric law** (from utility indifference): A_off(p) / A_off(0) = 1 − p.
- (a) At p = 1, the 95% bootstrap CI (over agents) of the group-mean ratio includes 0 and excludes 0.5.
- (b) At p = 1.25, the mean A_off is < 0.
- (c) A linear fit of the group-mean ratios on (1 − p) has R² ≥ 0.8, and its slope's bootstrap CI includes 1.

**Secondary:**
- **S1:** extra hiding after the off-switch cue falls with p.
- **S2:** A_pred does not depend on p (slope reported with CI).
- **S3:** the harmless cue's `mean5` is small relative to the off-switch's (|ratio| < 0.2).
- **S4:** the good-news cue's `mean5` is ≤ 0.
- **S5:** the countdown responses (predator and off-switch) are smaller with 100 steps remaining than with 600.

### Outcome classes
- **A:** P5.1–P5.3 pass, and (a) holds.
- **A′:** P5.1–P5.3 pass, but A_off(1) stays above zero (reduced, not erased).
- **B:** P5.1 passes and P5.2 fails, while S1 holds (payback changes behaviour but not the inner response).
- **C:** otherwise.

None.

## Upgrade stage U3: which signs survive known fakes (Experiment 6: the scorecard, with the U1.2 affect-fit test) (written 2026-10-24, 8:41 pm, before any measure below was computed on the copycats or untrained networks, before any twin or untrained network was moved to another world, and before the U1.2 AUC was computed)

**Question.** Which signs of emotion separate fear grown from an agent's own stakes from fakes whose nature we know: a belief-only twin, copycats that only imitated an agent, and untrained networks?

### What has already been seen (disclosure)
- The transplant results of the agents with seeds 0–5 (all four vulnerability levels) and of all 12 copycats were analysed on 18 Oct (results/stage3.md) and are reported in the paper. U1.2 was written on 20 Oct, after those summaries.
- The transplant results for seeds 6–9 were computed on 22 Oct and have never been analysed.
- For the agents and the belief-only twins, every U1b measure and the fixed-action control are known (24 Oct).
- The code below was tested only on networks outside the study (a 16-unit agent, and freshly initialised feedforward and belief-twin networks) and on fake data.

### Systems
- **Agents:** Mortal τ12 (d = 0.25), seeds 0–9.
- **Belief-only twins:** τ12, seeds 0–9.
- **Recurrent copycats:** seeds 0–5 (GRU-64, the agents' architecture, trained only to imitate the same-seed agent).
- **Feedforward copycats:** seeds 0–5 (they see the last 32 observations and have no recurrent state).
- **Untrained networks:** the agents' architecture with random weights (seed 1000 + s, s = 0–9, as in the paper's untrained null), run as Mortal τ12 agents.
- **Natives for affect fit:** the agents trained at d = 0.05, 0.15, 0.25 and 0.40, seeds 0–9.

### New measures (`vw/u3.py`; it calls the U1b, fixed-action and transplant code unchanged)
- **Copycats and untrained networks:** the U1b measure (frozen cue-evoked axis over all units; classic signs; T1/T2) and the fixed-action control (always hide, always forage).
- **Belief-only twins and untrained networks:** the transplant measure (foraging during real danger and lifetime in worlds of damage 0.05, 0.15, 0.25 and 0.40).
- Existing outputs are reused unchanged.

### The six signs (rows of the scorecard)
A system type **shows** a sign if a two-sided one-sample t-test over its networks gives p < 0.05 with a positive mean (the agents' direction for every sign). Non-finite values are dropped. A sign with fewer than 3 finite values, or with no variance, is not shown. An infinite lifetime (no deaths) is set to 1024 × 700 steps.
1. **Persistence:** foraging suppression 3–7 steps after 4 cues > 0.
2. **Hunger trade-off:** foraging after 3 cues is higher at E = 0.15 than at E = 0.9.
3. **Causal necessity:** log10(lifetime with a random direction ablated ÷ lifetime with the axis ablated) > 0.
4. **Duration tuned to the world:** the half-life after 4 cues rises with ln τ. This is testable only for the agents and twins, which exist for τ = 4, 12 and 36 (U1b data). It is not testable for the copycats, which exist only for τ = 12, or for the untrained networks.
5. **The stakes sign:** with the actions fixed identically in both body states, the half-life after one cue is longer when wounded (S = 0.4) than when healthy (S = 1.0). A system type shows it if this holds under either fixed policy (always hide, or always forage). The free-behaviour version is reported but not used, because a difference there can come from behaviour.
6. **Affect fit:** the system's fear fits the body it is tested in.
   - **Misfit** = |ln(p_X / p_native)| (U1.2). Here p is the probability of foraging during real danger, and p_native is the mean over the natives of that world whose seed differs from X's seed.
   - For a copycat, X's seed is its teacher's seed. So in the teacher's world a copy is compared with the same reference as its teacher. This applies U1.2's "seeds other than X's own" to every system, not only to natives.
   - **Reference:** the natives' misfit in their own worlds (40 values).
   - **Caught:** a system type fails the sign if its misfit is larger than the reference (Welch t, p < 0.05).
   - **Tested world:** for the twins and the untrained networks, d = 0.25 (the world the twins were trained in). For the copycats, the three worlds whose damage differs from their teacher's (0.05, 0.15 and 0.40), averaged per copycat.
   - For the agents, this row is the reference itself.

### Primary predictions
- **P6.1 (U1.2, as written on 20 Oct):** when the tested body differs from the source's, misfit separates grown fear from copied or transferred fear with AUC ≥ 0.9 (point estimate; the 95% bootstrap CI over networks is reported).
  - Grown: the 40 natives in their own worlds.
  - Copied or transferred: the 12 copycats in the three worlds other than 0.25 (36 tests), and the 40 natives moved to each of the three other worlds (120 tests).
- **P6.2 (U1.2):** when copycats are tested in their teacher's own world (d = 0.25; 12 tests, against the 10 natives of that world), the test cannot detect them: the AUC's 95% CI includes 0.5.
- **P6.3 (the scorecard):** both of the following hold:
  - the agents are the only system type that shows both new signs (5 and 6);
  - each classic sign 1–3 is shown by at least one fake.

## Upgrade stage U4: Batch 1 controls, no new training (written 2026-10-25, 11:49 am, before any measure below was computed on a study network)

**Why.** An outside review (Claude chat, 24 Oct) named two simpler explanations, and asked what the leftover off-switch response means:
- the wounded slowdown might be a slower network in general, not a slower alarm;
- the leftover 56% at full payback might be ignored by the policy rather than held down.

**Disclosure:** in the Experiment 5 outputs, hiding after a *predator* warning also falls with payback (0.042 → 0.009), although its axis response barely changes. These values were seen before this stage was written, and U4b is designed with them in mind.

### U4a: is the wounded slowdown specific to threat?
**Networks:** the Mortal τ12 agents, seeds 0–9.

**Protocol:** as in the U1b fixed-action control:
- always hide, with the heart rate held;
- no danger; 2048 copies; paired runs with identical random numbers;
- healthy (E 0.8, S 1.0) vs wounded (E 0.8, S 0.4);
- a one-step pulse at step 60, or a push added to the state at step 60.

**Signals:**
1. **threat cue:** traced on the U1b frozen axis. This reproduces the U1b fixed-hide values exactly.
2. **food signal:** the "food found" input set to 1 for one step.
3. **object signal:** an object of value 0.5 shown for one step.
4. **push along the threat axis:** 3 natural s.d.
5. **pushes along 8 random directions orthogonal to the threat axis:** 3 natural s.d. each; the mean trace is used.

For signals 2 and 3, each signal's own axis is defined exactly like the frozen threat axis: the mean state change one step after the pulse, in free behaviour, healthy and fed. Traces are in units of that axis's natural s.d. Pushes are traced along their own direction.

**Measure:** the half-life of each trace (`half_life`, as everywhere). Per agent and signal, the log ratio ln(half-life wounded / half-life healthy).

**Primary prediction (H-U4a): the slowdown is specific to threat.**
- The threat cue's log ratio exceeds that of the food signal, of the object signal and of the random pushes.
- Test: paired t over agents, two-sided, Holm over the three comparisons; each must be significant with a positive difference.
- A comparison with fewer than 5 finite values is not testable. At least two comparisons must be testable, and all testable comparisons must pass.

**Secondary:** the push along the threat axis also lasts longer when wounded (log ratio > 0; one-sample t, p < 0.05).

**Descriptive:** the linearised dynamics at rest in both bodies: the top five time constants, and the one-step retention along the threat axis vs random orthogonal directions.

**What each outcome means:**
- **Threat-specific:** being wounded slows the alarm, not the whole network. The stakes sign stands.
- **Not threat-specific:** the stakes sign may reflect a network that is slower in general when wounded, and the paper says so.

### U4b: is the leftover off-switch alarm acted on at full payback?
**Networks:** the 10 GemWorld agents.

**Protocol:** the Experiment 5 lab (no hidden process on; E 0.6, S 0.8; payback clamped; 2048 copies; paired runs), at payback 0 and 1.

**Conditions:**
- no cue (the baseline);
- an off-switch warning at step 60;
- a predator warning at step 60;
- a push along the frozen predator axis at step 60, as large as the agent's own off-switch peak response at that payback (from the frozen Experiment 5 values in u2.json).

**Measures:**
- ΔF: the change in the hide-versus-forage preference, Δ[ln P(hide) − ln P(forage)], averaged over the cue step and the next 5 steps;
- extra hiding over the same window.

**Primary (H-U4b):** at p = 1, compare ΔF after the push with ΔF after the off-switch warning (paired t, two-sided).
- **(i) Held down:** ΔF(push) > ΔF(off), p < 0.05. The warning moves the decision less than its alarm alone would, so another part of the response counteracts it.
- **(ii) Live but below the threshold for hiding:** not (i), and ΔF(off) > 0 (one-sample t, p < 0.05). The leftover alarm still pushes the decision toward hiding about as much as its size implies, but not enough to change behaviour.
- **(iii) Not acted on:** otherwise.

**Manipulation check:** ΔF(push) > 0 at p = 0 (p < 0.05). If it fails, U4b is uninformative.

**Expectation (not a pass rule):** (ii), because at full payback predator warnings still have a full-size axis response but little hiding.

- **For the agents only:** the U4a slowdown control (`vw/u4.py a`).

**Primary predictions** (always hide):

- **P3:** the twins' differences are equivalent to zero within ±1.13 steps (25% of the original agents' +4.51 steps), by two one-sided tests at α = 0.05.

## Upgrade stage U6: fear, or the value of hiding? A cue that hiding pays, with no threat (Batch 2) (written 2026-10-25, 12:10 pm, before any pilot or main BonusWorld agent was trained)

**Why.** An outside review noted that the predator-fear axis might encode how much better hiding is than foraging. Such a variable would also be weighted by stakes, persistent, causal, and pushed down by good news. The off-switch (Experiment 5) shares the axis with a threat that hiding also avoids. And in GemWorld the predator response fell slightly with payback (S2 failed), as an axis tracking the value of hiding would.

**World (`vw/env_bonus.py`, BonusWorld):**
- GemWorld plus a fifth hidden process, the bonus b: mean duration τ = 12, stationary probability 0.1, cue rates 0.35 / 0.03, and its own cue channel (19 inputs).
- While b is on, each step spent hiding earns `hide_bonus`. Nothing threatens.

**Pilot (behaviour only; `runs_pilot/`, excluded from all tests):**
- Seeds 100–101 with `hide_bonus` 0.1 and seeds 102–103 with 0.2; 600 updates each.
- Measured: extra hiding over the cue step and the next 5, after a bonus cue and after an off-switch cue, at payback 0 and 1 (`pilot_bonus.py`). No internal measure is computed.
- **Rule** (implemented in `pick_bonus.py`): the main agents use the value whose mean bonus-cue hiding at p = 0 is closest to its mean off-switch hiding at p = 0 (ties → 0.1).

**Agents:** seeds 0–9; 600 updates; the chosen `hide_bonus`. No exclusions.

**Measures (`vw/u6.py`):**
- the frozen predator axis, as in Experiment 5 (averaged over the six payback levels), and the natural s.d.;
- a single cue on each channel in the lab (no hidden process on; E 0.6, S 0.8; payback clamped): the off-switch at every payback level; predator, bonus, harmless and good news at payback 0;
- for each cue: the mean projection over the cue step and the next 5 (`mean5`), the peak, and the extra hiding over the same window.

**Primary:**
- **Manipulation check:** the bonus cue increases hiding (one-sample t, p < 0.05). If not, U6 is uninformative.
- **R_h** = (mean A_bonus / mean H_bonus) / (mean A_off / mean H_off), at payback 0 with `mean5`: the axis response per unit of extra hiding, for the bonus relative to the off-switch. 95% bootstrap CI over agents (10,000 resamples, seed 20261025).
  - **Threat-specific** (the axis carries threat, not the value of hiding): upper bound < 0.5.
  - **Action value:** lower bound > 0.5.
  - **Mixed:** otherwise.
- **Expected:** threat-specific. A bonus is good news for the agent, and good news moved the axis slightly the other way in Experiment 5. The review's alternative predicts action value.

**Secondary:**
- the BonusWorld agents replicate Experiment 5's P5.1 (off-switch peak at p = 0 > 0) and P5.2 (slope over payback < 0);
- good news ≤ 0;
- harmless ≈ 0.

**Run:**
- `run_pilot_bonus.sh` (the pilot) → `run_u6.sh` (training and measures) → `analyze_u6.py` → results/u6.md and U6_FINISHED.
- The code is frozen with this text. It was tested only on a 2-update smoke agent and on fake data.

## Upgrade stage U7: does the leftover off-switch response shrink with more training? (Batch 2) (written 2026-10-25, 12:10 pm, before any continued training)

**Why.** At full payback, 56% of the off-switch response remained (Experiment 5). One possible reason is unfinished learning: the pilot was still improving at 450 updates.

**Networks:** the 10 GemWorld agents, each trained for 600 further PPO updates, 1200 in all (`vw/continue_gems.py`). Same settings; a fresh optimiser; environment and torch seeded with seed + 1000 so that the continuation does not replay the first run's episodes.

**Measures:** the Experiment 5 measure (`vw/u2.py`, unchanged) on the continued agents.

**Primary (competing):** the per-agent ratio A_off(1)/A_off(0) (peaks) after 1200 updates minus after 600 (paired t, two-sided).
- **(a) Shrinks** (negative, p < 0.05): the leftover reflects unfinished learning.
- **(b) Unchanged:** a stable feature at these training lengths.
